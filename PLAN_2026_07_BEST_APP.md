# PLAN: Best Park-Companion App in the World — July 2026 Multi-Agent Audit

Produced 2026-07-05 by a 9-agent audit (6 code lenses over app+backend+internal docs, 2 market research agents, 1 synthesis). Mission: complement and make people's park trips more fun. Ideas/plan only — no code changed.

## What the app is today
Today this is a feature-rich, pre-revenue Pokemon GO-for-theme-parks companion covering 11 hardcoded US Disney/Universal parks: a server-authoritative map spawns coins, tasks, pins, and gym swords that players collect by physically walking the park, backed by a genuinely production-grade GPS ride journal, a forum, TPS news, and an unusually deep meta layer (streaks, sets, stamps, leaderboards, teams). The engineering pattern is right (server decides everything, content updates ship without app releases via Nova admin and crumbs), and the retention design rivals funded studios. But it earns zero dollars (both the purchases SDK and ads are no-op stubs), cannot reach lapsed users (no push), cannot see itself (no analytics or crash reporting), and its flagship differentiator, background ride auto-detection, is silently dead in production builds because the native Info.plist lacks background-location entitlements. The owner's own four audit passes from March 2026 correctly diagnosed the app as three products in a trench coat with a clear thesis (park exploration + ride memory + park-native social), then zero of those recommendations shipped; the only work since was a 3D queue-game spike that violates the audits' own "more loops is not more retention" warning. It is a strong engine with the fuel line disconnected.

## What it does well
- Server-authoritative core loop: spawn windows, proximity checks (/me/current-redeemable), and all redemptions validated backend-side inside DB transactions. The correct architecture for a location game; the client is mostly a renderer.
- Ride detection is production-grade: background task with AsyncStorage queue and write mutex, confidence scoring against live ThemeParks.wiki wait times, batch confirm UX, nothing auto-logs without user consent. This is a shippable standalone product.
- Retention game design is deeper than most funded competitors: server-driven daily gifts, streak model with at-risk flag, collections (pins/stamps/sets/coin shelf), two leaderboards, friends, teams, shareable Wrapped cards.
- Server-driven content ops: copy (crumbs), themes, store rotations, park content all editable in Nova admin with no app release. This is the backbone that makes media-synced live-ops possible.
- Real deployment pipeline: 4 EAS profiles, OTA updates actively published (June 30), automated ASC submit, New Architecture already enabled.
- Exceptional self-knowledge: four audit passes with file:line evidence and a defensible product thesis. The vision work is done; only execution is missing.
- Best-possible fantasy fit per market research: the park IS the magical location (the exact thing Wizards Unite lacked), and park collecting culture (pins, pressed pennies, hidden Mickeys) has pre-trained the audience for decades.

## Critical foundations (fix before features)
- Zero revenue infrastructure (known internally, still blocker #1 for the $100k MRR filter): adapty-stub returns an empty product list so the paywall renders blank, ads-stub means no ad ever shows, and the fake 3-second WatchAd hands every free user the subscriber-tier 2x perk, gutting the VIP value prop. Reinstall a real purchases SDK and kill fake doubling before anything else.
- No push notifications: expo-notifications is absent from package.json entirely. The server already computes streak_at_risk and daily gifts but has no delivery channel. For an episodic-visit app this is the single biggest missing retention lever; win-back, streak saves, and drop-day announcements are all impossible today.
- Flying blind: no analytics SDK, no crash reporting, and LogBox suppresses network/promise-rejection errors. Retention, funnel, paywall conversion, and crashes are all invisible. Nothing downstream can be evaluated until this exists.
- Background ride detection is dead in production: ios/ThemeParkShark/Info.plist lacks UIBackgroundModes and NSLocationAlwaysAndWhenInUseUsageDescription, and bare workflow ignores app.config.js, so the flagship auto-log feature fails with a silent console.warn. Hours to fix, currently shipping broken.
- Battery abuse: foreground watcher at BestForNavigation with 1m distance filter, a second simultaneous watcher during detection, a heading stream, a fallback poll, and keep-awake. Market research is unambiguous: battery drain is the #1 delete trigger for park apps (Play Disney Parks' documented flaw). This kills the app by 2pm on the exact day it matters.
- Backend hardening (new findings, not in the internal audits): APP_DEBUG=true in production leaks stack traces and env values; throttle:api is commented out (unthrottled login and coin-code brute force); QUEUE_CONNECTION=sync makes Horizon dead weight; LogRequests persists Apple identity tokens and plaintext Sanctum tokens to the DB on every login; double_xp/double_coins are client-sent booleans the server applies without checking subscription. Config-level fixes, catastrophic if left.
- GPS trust is zero: current_park_id is set from client-supplied lat/lng with no attestation, mock-location check, or plausibility (speed/teleport) check. Anyone can farm every collectible from a couch. This undermines the one thing the whole product certifies (you were there), and any future presence-based value (souvenirs, sponsorships).
- Offline fragility in the primary usage context: both WebView minigames load three.js from unpkg and fonts from Google at runtime, and there is no redeem retry queue, so weak in-park cellular means a broken game at the moment of peak intent.
- Execute the March audit backlog before adding anything: 14 flagged dead files still ship, both fail-open economy bugs remain in RedeemRedeemableModal, master is 5+ months stale behind feat/queue-mini-games-3d, and the 4-domain IA consolidation never started. The highest-ROI 'new work' is the owner's own to-do list.

## Quick wins (days, not weeks)
- Backend config flips, same day: APP_DEBUG=false, uncomment throttle:api, QUEUE_CONNECTION=redis, redact LogRequests so it stops persisting Apple identity tokens and plaintext Sanctum tokens.
- Add UIBackgroundModes ['location'] and the Always usage string to ios/ThemeParkShark/Info.plist: hours of work, un-breaks the flagship background ride detection.
- Derive double_xp/double_coins server-side from is_subscribed and ignore the client booleans; closes a standing economy exploit in one controller change.
- Fix the two known fail-open bugs in RedeemRedeemableModal (ticket spend proceeds on API error, task completion synthesizes local rewards on error); flagged in the owner's own PASS2 audit, still shipping.
- Add Sentry plus a minimal analytics SDK with ~10 core events (session, park detected, redeem, task win, paywall view, share). Everything else in this plan is unmeasurable without it.
- Ship the built-but-dead streak UI: DailyLoginCalendar, StreakBadge, StreakFlame exist, are polished, and are imported by nothing. Free D7 retention work already paid for.
- Bundle three.js and fonts locally into the WebView minigames; they currently fail on weak park cellular, the app's primary context.
- Add an expo-store-review prompt after PostWinRewards or a confirmed ride log; zero review volume is being captured at the app's happiest moments.
- Merge feat/queue-mini-games-3d to master, delete the four stale claude/* branches, and run the PASS3 dead-file sweep (14 files) in one commit.

## Big ideas (ranked)

### 1. Park Day Recap [medium]
Park Day Recap: an auto-generated end-of-day keepsake (rides ridden via GPS detection, route walked, collectibles found, park coins earned) rendered as a shareable postcard the moment you leave the park geofence, with a nightly push ('your day at EPCOT is ready').

**Why:** Directly serves the mission: the trip itself becomes a souvenir. Pikmin Bloom's Daily Lookback proves the peak-end mechanic; market research calls 'no memory artifact' wide-open whitespace against the $185 Memory Maker; every building block already exists in the codebase (ride detection, park_visits pivot, ViewShot ShareableRideCard, RideWrapped patterns). Internal docs have annual Wrapped only, not a per-visit recap. Every share is a TPS-branded asset the 312K IG account can repost, making users the marketing department.

### 2. TPS Drop Days [medium]
TPS Drop Days: time-boxed collectible drops (3-hour windows, one exclusive date-locked souvenir at a specific land or ride) announced through TPS Instagram, articles, and push, synchronized with real park news (new ride opens, festival starts, Epic Universe anniversary).

**Why:** Better angle on the already-planned 'monthly rotating sets' backend: rotation alone is content, but media-synchronized scarcity is appointment behavior (Pokemon GO Community Day) plus permanent presence-certified value (Geocaching date-locked souvenirs), and it solves the repeat-visit staleness pain for passholders. No competitor has a 312K-follower news outlet as its event calendar; this is the one mechanic where the distribution advantage compounds instead of just acquiring installs. Server-driven spawns mean drops need no app release.

### 3. Own Epic Universe [small]
Own Epic Universe: make it the single best-covered park in the app (densest tasks, exclusive collectible set, opening-year date-locked stamp), and cross-link every TPS Epic Universe guide and article to the app as 'the Epic Universe game'.

**Why:** Research identifies Epic Universe as the newest, most explorable park in America with zero companion play layer from Universal, and Play Disney Parks will never cover it. TPS already ranks for Epic content (SEO is the brand's stated priority). This is pure content work in Nova admin plus editorial, no client code, and it gives the app a beachhead identity ('the app for Epic') instead of competing head-on at Disney where Play Disney is entrenched.

### 4. Queue Mode reboot [medium]
Queue Mode reboot: kill both 3D spikes (Filament wireframe cube, CDN-dependent WebView) and instead auto-detect standing in a line, then serve a queue session sized to the actual posted wait (already fetched from ThemeParks.wiki): passive ride-part accrual via the existing InLineTimer backend, the existing 5 mini-games bundled offline, solo-playable, one-thumb, battery-light.

**Why:** Better angle on planned ROADMAP_V2 Priority 4: the moat is queue-aware context, not 3D rendering (the current spike added a third rendering stack and violates PASS4's own 'more loops' warning). Queueing is the #1 guest complaint industry-wide; Play Disney Parks' documented gaps are battery drain, Disney-only coverage, and multiplayer-required games. InLineTimer endpoints already exist; this is assembly, not invention.

### 5. Presence-certified Park Passport [medium]
Presence-certified Park Passport: replace the quarantined XP-goal StampBook with auto-awarded, date-stamped visit artifacts per park ('Magic Kingdom, July 5 2026') plus seasonal and event variants, displayed as a family trip log that spans Disney, Universal, and future parks.

**Why:** Better angle on a system PASS4 quarantined: the stamp concept fails as a chore list but wins as certification. Geocaching sustained 25 years of paying retention on exactly this mechanic; Gowalla proved the passport metaphor needs zero explanation for families; research flags cross-park continuity ('our family's park passport') as having no owner. Requires the GPS-trust fix first, which is what makes certification mean something.

### 6. Buddy Shark [large]
Buddy Shark: collapse the two competing customization systems into one persistent companion you feed prep items (the churro economy) at home, leveling friendship tiers that unlock cosmetics, map presence, and recap cameos.

**Why:** Answers the audits' open question (legacy inventory vs V2 cosmetics) by making the choice for a reason: Pokemon GO's buddy system is documented as 'doing all the lifting' for daily retention because one persistent companion beats a hundred interchangeable collectibles for habit formation. It gives Travel Mode's prep items the 'why' PASS4 asked for (gear up your shark for the next park day) and finally deletes the orphaned SharkCustomizationScreen.

## Build sequence
1. Phase 0 (week 1), Stop the bleeding: all quick wins above, prioritizing backend security flips, token-log redaction, Info.plist background location, server-side reward multipliers, Sentry + analytics, and repo hygiene. Rationale: everything downstream is either insecure, invisible, or built on a stale branch until this lands.
2. Phase 1 (weeks 2-4), Revenue and retention rails: reinstall a real purchases SDK and make the VIP paywall transact, move the intro paywall from post-team-selection to after the first core-loop win (audit flags current placement as riskiest), make rewarded ads real or remove the doubling UX entirely, and ship expo-notifications with three flows: streak-at-risk, daily gift, and lapsed win-back. Rationale: the $100k MRR filter demands a working till, and push must exist before spending TPS distribution on installs you cannot re-engage.
3. Phase 2 (month 2), Park-day reliability: battery tuning (accuracy tiers by context, geofence-gate the aggressive watcher, kill keep-awake), offline redeem retry queue, and server-side GPS plausibility checks (speed/teleport sanity, mock-location flagging). Rationale: research says battery and reliability are the delete triggers, and presence integrity is a prerequisite for the keepsake layer's value.
4. Phase 3 (months 2-3), The keepsake layer: ship Park Day Recap and the presence-certified Passport, and reposition the app's public pitch via TPS as 'the free ride journal and park passport'. Rationale: this is the whitespace no incumbent occupies, it produces the viral share asset, and it executes PASS4's ride-memory pillar instead of adding a new loop.
5. Phase 4 (month 3+), Turn on the flywheel: Epic Universe content beachhead, first TPS Drop Day synced to a real park news moment, referral deep links with an invite reward replacing the bare share sheet. Rationale: only spend the 312K-follower cannon once the product retains, converts, and shares; then every TPS post compounds.
6. Phase 5 (later), Depth and breadth: Queue Mode reboot, Buddy Shark consolidation, Expo SDK 52-to-57 catch-up, then Android and regional-park expansion (server-driven park config replacing the two hardcoded lists). Rationale: these are the large bets, and each gets dramatically cheaper and safer once analytics, revenue, and the keepsake identity are proven.

## Appendix: research evidence

### mechanics
- Pokemon GO Community Day (3-hour time-boxed event, one featured collectible with an exclusive reward): works because scarcity is scheduled and knowable, so it creates appointment behavior instead of anxiety; everyone hunting the same thing at the same time makes the crowd itself the content, and the exclusive move/variant makes attendance feel permanently valuable (missing it = a hole in your collection). Park translation: a 'collectible drop' tied to a specific land + time window turns FOMO into a reason to cross the park.
- Pokemon GO Raids (co-op bosses requiring physically co-located players): peer-reviewed research (NCBI/Cyberpsychology studies) shows the in-person cooperative modes are the ones that measurably increase well-being; strangers become community, and lapsed players return because friends need bodies for the raid. The retention driver is other people, not the boss.
- Pokemon GO Buddy System (pick one creature, feed/walk/photograph it through 4 friendship tiers): works via pet attachment + sunk-cost investment; per MassivelyOP it is 'doing all the lifting' for daily retention because it gives 4-6 tiny guaranteed-reward interactions per day. A single persistent companion beats a hundred interchangeable collectibles for habit formation.
- Pikmin Bloom passive step progress (steps accrue while the app is closed; opening the app converts them into rewards): zero-friction progress during normal life means every app open is a guaranteed payoff, which is the cleanest habit loop in the genre. Perfect fit for a park day where guests already walk 5-10 miles; the walking they must do anyway becomes the game.
- Pikmin Bloom Daily Lookback + postcards (end-of-day replay of your route, collectible postcards from places visited): exploits the peak-end rule; converts location history into a keepsake and a shareable artifact. Nobody else in the park space does 'your day as a souvenir' well; this is a wide-open steal.
- Geocaching souvenirs + streaks (virtual art locked to being at a specific place on a specific date, displayed on profile): 25 years of retention on the back of set-completion psychology and pilgrimage behavior; because the souvenir can ONLY be earned by physical presence, it certifies the trip. Date-locked park souvenirs ('Epic Universe, opening summer') would work identically.
- Gowalla passport + stamps (every check-in = a stamp in a personal passport): the passport metaphor is instantly legible with zero explanation needed, and it frames collection as a personal travel log rather than competition, which suits casual/family users. Gowalla's item drops + 'founder' status also created serendipity and status-through-discovery.
- Randonautica intention-setting + random coordinates: manufactured serendipity plus confirmation bias means users write their own story at the destination, and every trip becomes a postable narrative (10.8M users almost entirely from TikTok self-propagation). Park translation: 'mystery quest' mechanics that send guests to an unexpected corner generate UGC for free.
- Play Disney Parks queue-synced games (games that only function inside a specific standby line, with physical queue details that activate in-app, e.g. Space Mountain symbols, Peter Pan trinket hunt): attacks the #1 guest complaint (queueing) by converting dead time into content, and hiding triggers in the physical queue rewards looking UP at the environment instead of down at the phone. Reviewers call the polish the app's best feature.
- MagicBand+ / Batuu Bounty Hunters (wave at physical character statues to 'collect' them; wearable lights/haptics as the interface): heads-up, screen-light physical-digital bridging is the right instinct; the collect-by-gesture moment is the joy. The lesson is the direction (body in space, not eyes on screen), while its execution flaws (battery, flaky gesture recognition) are the anti-pattern.
- Museum scavenger-hunt trails (unlock clues on arrival at a gallery, badges per themed mission): proven to convert passive viewing into active participation and extend dwell time; ACM research on teen visitors warns task complexity kills fun, so quests must be one-step simple. Design rule: the device should point at the place, never compete with it.
- IP/fantasy fit as a mechanic (Pokemon GO's real lesson): the game must make you feel like the fantasy in that location; Pokemon spawning in a park is coherent, wizard fortresses in a playground were not (PocketGamer's Wizards Unite postmortem). A theme-park collectible game has the best possible fantasy fit, since the park IS the magical location; nostalgia research also shows self-continuity ('being a kid again') directly drives well-being and stickiness.

### painPoints
- Queueing is the #1 guest complaint industry-wide (Merlin CEO), and the worst part is uncertainty, not just length; dead time in line with bored kids is the single biggest solvable moment of the day.
- Phone battery dying by noon: the phone is command central (tickets, maps, mobile order, photos) and location-services-heavy apps are a known killer; any park game MUST be battery-frugal or it gets deleted after day one. Guests already resent hunting for outlets or paying for charging.
- Official park apps fail at peak load exactly when needed: My Disney Experience outages block virtual queues and Lightning Lane purchases; Universal's app posts false 'closed for the day' statuses. Reliability + honest data is an open trust gap.
- No location awareness: MDE shows the entire resort instead of the park you're standing in, forcing multi-step filtering; guests want 'what's near me right now, what should I do next'.
- Planning stress and regret ('could smarter planning have avoided this 90-minute line'), plus the pressure of being the family's trip-logistics officer all day.
- Screen-time guilt: parents specifically praise the 'dumb' MagicBand for getting out of the way; a game that demands eyes-down attention fights the reason people came. Heads-up, glanceable, passive-progress mechanics win.
- No memory artifact: the day ends with a camera roll and a receipt; nothing turns the route walked, rides ridden, and things found into a keepsake (the Pikmin Bloom postcard/Lookback gap applied to parks).
- Repeat-visit staleness: locals and passholders have nothing new to hunt on visit 30; date/season-locked collectibles and rotating quests directly solve 'why come back in September'.
- Sunlight readability and disjointed UX in existing park apps (Universal's described as built by teams that never coordinated); low-friction, high-contrast, one-thumb design is a differentiator by itself.

### competitors
- My Disney Experience: owns logistics and commerce (tickets, mobile order, Lightning Lane) but is sluggish, crashes at peak, has bad search, and no joy layer; it is a wallet, not a game. Nobody opens it happily.
- Play Disney Parks: the gold standard for queue entertainment; polished, responsive, with physical-queue easter eggs. Weaknesses to exploit: trivia repeats after ~2 plays per land, it is a battery hog, it is nearly useless outside standby lines, and there is no persistent collection/meta that carries across visits or parks.
- Universal Orlando app: functional baseline but posts inaccurate ride statuses (AllEars: 'keeps lying to you'), omits POIs from the map, crashes on payment flows; zero game layer at Epic Universe despite it being the newest, most explorable park in America. Big whitespace.
- MagicBand+ with Batuu Bounty Hunters: proves guests will pay $30+ for physical-digital interaction and love collect-by-gesture moments, but is hurt by charging burden, battery drain on both band and phone, and a weak value story vs. the free phone. Lesson: the interaction model is validated, the hardware dependency is the liability.
- Wait-time utilities (Queue-Times, Thrill Data, park.fan): accurate crowd data and predictions that enthusiasts trust more than official apps, but pure utility with zero emotional retention; a companion that pairs their honesty with a collection game outflanks both them and the official apps.
- Geocaching (geocaching.com): the longevity proof; 25 years of retention from souvenirs, streaks, and profile trophies on a tiny budget, showing place-locked collectibles sustain a paying subscriber base without AR gimmicks.
- Dead competitors as cautionary tales: Wizards Unite ($37M lifetime vs Pokemon GO's $1B/yr) died from IP-mechanic mismatch, cloned systems, and no reason to collect; Gowalla died from ever-morphing, over-complicated mechanics and redesigns that deleted the features users loved (Foursquare won on simple, legible rules); Minecraft Earth (<$500k lifetime) died demanding outdoor play with no passive fallback; NBA All-World and Catan World Explorers died proving a brand pasted on the GO formula is worthless without fantasy fit. Anti-pattern summary: complexity, mechanic churn, screen-first design, battery abuse, and collecting-without-a-why are the five killers.

### mechanics
- Presence-gated scarcity (Pokemon GO): spawns and regional exclusives you can only get by physically standing somewhere make GPS itself the loot mechanic. It works because 'being there' becomes the achievement, and the collectible is proof. Counter-lesson from 2025 backlash: remote raid passes and whale-priced events diluted place-based scarcity and players noticed. Never sell the collectible directly, sell presence.
- Streaks and habit loops (Pokemon GO daily catch/spin, Munzee monthly deploy streaks): loss aversion keeps players opening the app. For a park game, daily streaks fail because visits are episodic; the port is per-visit streaks and 'visit N parks this year' chains that survive gaps between trips.
- Passport + stamps (Gowalla relaunch 2023): every check-in stamps a passport and can drop a spot-specific virtual item. Works because the passport metaphor is instantly legible to non-gamers and kids, and each stamp is a permanent artifact of a real day. Gowalla proved the metaphor but never specialized in parks.
- Virtual souvenirs (Geocaching souvenirs, Munzee badges): a graphic awarded for being at a place on a date. Works because it converts an ephemeral visit into a permanent, displayable object with zero marginal cost. Parks are the single best-fit venue for this mechanic and nobody has built it cross-park.
- Rarity tiers and flexible play (Munzee): different munzee types carry different point values, plus clans and badges, so hardcore and casual players coexist. Works because completionists chase rare types while casuals still get dopamine from commons. Maps directly to common/rare/event-limited park collectibles.
- Complement, don't compete (Pikmin Bloom): deliberately low-attention 'walking buddy' design that rewards the activity you were already doing instead of demanding heads-down play. Engadget-documented multi-year retention. This is THE design law for a park game: the rides are the show, the app must be glanceable and battery-frugal (Play Disney Parks' battery drain is a cautionary tale).
- Lifelog + postcards (Pikmin Bloom): end-of-day auto-recap of where you walked, a one-line journal, and shareable postcard keepsakes from places visited. Works because it manufactures nostalgia from the day you just had, and shared postcards double as organic distribution. A park version (auto day-timeline, ride log, digital postcard per land) attacks the $185 Memory Maker pain from below.
- Location-gated queue games (Play Disney Parks): games that unlock ONLY while physically in a specific standby line make the wait itself the venue and the exclusivity exciting. Proven concept with known flaws to fix: battery drain, crashes, many games need multiple players, nothing for Lightning Lane users, Disney-only. Solo-playable, cross-park queue content is open space.
- Credit counting (Coaster-Count, Coaster Creds, RCDB-backed): coaster enthusiasts already self-organize around lifetime 'credits,' re-ride counts, stats, and family accounts managed from one login. Works because it feeds identity and completionism with a lifetime log. Whitespace: these tools are utilitarian, adult-oriented, not GPS-verified, and have no game loop; a kid-friendly gamified ride log rides an organic behavior that already exists.
- Physical collecting culture already trained the audience (pin trading, pressed pennies, Disney's 2026 Mickey's Park Rangers hunt that ends in a stamped book and sticker, Hidden Mickeys with 4,750+ cataloged spots, MouseWait's in-park sticker nights): parks have spent decades teaching visitors to collect and to hunt hidden location-anchored details. A GPS collectible layer digitizes existing behavior instead of inventing a new one, and hidden-detail hunts are already what parents improvise in line.
- Appointment social events (Pokemon GO raids/Community Days): scheduled same-place-same-time moments create real-world gatherings and urgency. Park port: limited-window event collectibles (opening day, seasonal overlays, festival drops) that reward being at THIS park THIS weekend. Also aligns with parks' own interest in distributing crowds.
- Permissionless placement via GPS zones: Munzee requires deploying physical QR codes (impossible inside parks without operator consent), while pure GPS geofences need no park cooperation. Third parties like Touring Plans and Queue-Times have operated on top of parks for years without permission. Virtual-only collectibles keep that freedom; the constraint is IP hygiene (original collectibles, not Disney characters).

### painPoints
- Waiting in line is the defining dead time of a park day: crowding/wait-time worry ties at 25% among top visitor concerns (Kinetic319 2025 survey), and queues are captive 30-90 minute windows where phones come out anyway.
- Kids get bored and melt down in line: parents' current toolkit is analog hacks (I Spy, 20 Questions, bubbles, snacks, glow sticks per Scary Mommy/Mom Rewritten roundups). Play Disney Parks only covers select Disney standby queues, drains battery, and most games need multiple players. Nothing exists for Universal, Six Flags, or regional parks, and nothing solo-playable for a single bored kid.
- Cost anxiety poisons the day: 35%+ call ticket prices the biggest buzzkill, 24% of visitors took on debt for a Disney trip (45% among parents of young kids), and 65% say food cost far more than budgeted. Free added value inside the park (a game, souvenirs that cost nothing) directly improves the felt quality/price ratio, the most common complaint category.
- Remembering the trip is a premium upsell: Memory Maker is $185 advance / $210 in-park (up well past inflation, per DISboards threads), and there is no lightweight automatic record of 'what we actually did today' (rides ridden, lands walked, order of the day). Pikmin Bloom's lifelog proves appetite for auto-generated day recaps. A free auto ride-log/day-timeline/keepsake is wide-open whitespace.
- Official apps are unreliable and joyless: My Disney Experience crashes, forces re-logins (users report opening it 30+ times a day), splits plans confusingly, and died in the Oct 2025 AWS outage; Universal's app posts false 'closed for the day' ride statuses (AllEars, July 2025); Six Flags' Dec 2025 update geofenced wait times and demands always-on location. Visitors tolerate them as commerce utilities but there is zero delight, which is exactly the emotional gap a companion game fills.
- Battery is the scarcest resource of a park day: official apps get opened dozens of times, Play Disney Parks is notorious for drain, and a dead phone kills tickets, LL returns, and photos. Any GPS game that isn't aggressively battery-frugal (significant-location-change geofencing, no always-on AR) dies with the phone by 3pm.
- Planning is a part-time job and scheduling conflicts rank alongside crowds (25%): Touring Plans and crowd calendars serve the planner-parent before the trip, but nobody serves the kids DURING the day. The planning stack is saturated; the in-the-moment engagement layer is empty.
- Multi-park families have no continuity: a family that hits Disneyland in spring, a Six Flags in summer, and Universal in fall starts from zero in three different apps. Coaster counters solved this for adult enthusiasts only. A cross-park identity ('our family's park passport') has no owner.

### competitors
- My Disney Experience (official): does well LL booking, mobile order, PhotoPass integration, maps. Does badly: crashes, constant forced re-logins, plans split across confusing sections, redirects to web for park passes, single point of failure (AWS outage Oct 2025). Pure commerce/logistics, no play layer. Takeaway: don't compete on utility, Disney owns it; complement it.
- Play Disney Parks (official, Disney-only): the closest thing to the concept and the strongest validation. Polished location-gated queue games, trivia, achievements. Weaknesses to exploit: heavy battery drain, frequent crashes, only select Disney standby queues (nothing in Lightning Lane), most games require multiple players, achievements are siloed and don't become a lasting keepsake, and it will never cover Universal/Six Flags/regional parks.
- Universal Orlando app (official): easy to use, good mobile ordering and ticket wallet. Bad: chronically false 'closed for the day' ride statuses (AllEars documented pattern, July 2025), crashes, broken account linking. No game or collectible layer at all; Epic Universe opened May 2025 with zero companion play experience. Universal parks are underserved whitespace.
- Six Flags / regional park apps (official): actively hostile UX in 2025: wait times geofenced so you can't even check from home, location permission must be 'always,' non-interactive maps, reinstall-to-fix bugs. Regional parks are a software desert, and they're where repeat local visits (the natural habitat of a streak/collection game) actually happen.
- Touring Plans: the data gold standard (57M historical wait times, academic optimization models, crowd calendars) behind a subscription. Serves the planner-parent pre-trip. Zero kid engagement, zero in-day delight, zero memory keeping. Respect the moat, don't enter it.
- Thrill Data: free, deep historical wait/LL-price data, beloved by enthusiasts, but ad-cluttered, web-first, and strictly analytical. Confirms enthusiasts crave park data; confirms nobody is packaging park data as play.
- Queue-Times and Ride Ready: free live wait times across dozens of parks (Ride Ready adds freemium AI day-planning, alerts, walking directions across 15 parks including Universal and regionals). Proof that cross-park third-party infrastructure works and that parks tolerate it. Still pure utility; their existence means wait-time data can be a commodity input to a game rather than the product.
- MouseWait: 3M lifetime users, an active 27K community, real-world meetups and STICKER NIGHTS around Disneyland. Proves a third-party park community app can sustain itself for 15+ years and that in-park collectible culture (stickers) has organic pull. Aging, Disney-only, and social-first rather than game-first.
- Coaster-Count / Coaster Creds: lifetime coaster 'credit' logs, stats, re-rides, family member accounts under one login, RCDB-synced park databases. The organic collecting behavior already exists at scale in the enthusiast community. Gaps: no GPS verification, no game loop, no kids' design, no memories/keepsakes, enthusiast-brutalist UX.
- Gowalla / Munzee / Geocaching (generic location games): proven passport-stamp, badge, rarity, and souvenir mechanics, but no park content depth, and Munzee-style physical QR deployment is impossible inside parks without operator permission. Virtual GPS-zone collectibles inherit their mechanics without their placement problem.
- Net whitespace: a cross-park (Disney + Universal + Six Flags + regionals), kid-first, battery-frugal GPS collectible layer that (1) fills queue dead time with solo-playable location-gated hunts, (2) auto-generates a free keepsake of the day (stamp passport, ride log, postcards) undercutting $185 Memory Maker emotionally, (3) formalizes existing collecting culture (credits, pins, hidden details) for families instead of adult enthusiasts, and (4) follows Pikmin Bloom's complement-don't-compete law so it never fights the rides for attention. No incumbent occupies any of that intersection.
